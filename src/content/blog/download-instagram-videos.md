Instagram videos are easy to share but are still owned by their creators. Downloading makes sense for your own posts, client assets you are responsible for, or public content whose owner has authorized reuse.

## Get the canonical post link

Open the Reel or video post, use **Share**, and copy its link. Avoid copying a profile URL, search page, or temporary redirect. The downloader needs the specific public post address.

Private posts, disappearing content, and login-only media are not appropriate inputs for a public URL tool. Never give an Instagram password to a downloader.

## Confirm what you can do with the file

Permission to view is not necessarily permission to repost. Keep a record of the creator's approval, credit them when required, and do not remove a visible watermark or attribution.

## Use the web analyzer

Sign in to the site, paste the eligible Instagram URL, and select **Analyze**. If a format is returned, review the title and preview before starting the one-time free download.

The trial limit is one use per account, with a maximum of 720p, 10 minutes, and 500 MB. Choose carefully before starting the download.

## If the link cannot be analyzed

- Confirm the post is public and still available.
- Remove tracking text copied around the URL.
- Open the link in a private browser window to test public access.
- Recopy the canonical post link.
- Do not retry private or login-only content.

## Preserve useful context

Rename the file with the creator, campaign, and date. Store the source link and permission alongside the media. This makes later editing and attribution much safer than relying on memory.

## Prefer first-party exports for your account

For a full archive of your own Instagram activity, use Meta's official account-data export. It is more complete than downloading posts individually and can include supporting account information.

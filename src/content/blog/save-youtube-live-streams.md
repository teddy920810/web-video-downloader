Live streams behave differently from ordinary uploaded videos. The safest archive workflow is to use the platform's creator tools for your own broadcast or wait until an authorized public replay has finished processing.

## If you own the stream

Use YouTube Studio to manage and download your own completed broadcast when that option is available. Keep the original local recording as the master copy whenever possible; it usually preserves better quality than a later web download.

## If someone else owns it

Ask the creator for permission or use a download explicitly provided by them. Do not archive paid events, members-only broadcasts, or copyrighted streams without authorization.

## Wait for the replay to stabilize

After a live event ends, YouTube may need time to process the recording and its quality variants. Open the normal watch page and confirm that seeking works across the replay. A link that still shows a live-only state may not be ready for analysis.

## Check the web trial limits

The current trial accepts only short, eligible public videos: up to 10 minutes, 720p, and 500 MB. Most live replays are longer, so they will not fit. Do not split or repeatedly submit a protected broadcast to evade the limit.

The planned desktop product is the better future home for longer authorized workflows, but its final capabilities have not been announced.

## Choose an archive format

For an eligible short replay, MP4 offers broad compatibility. Keep a separate text file with the title, event date, creator, source URL, and permission record. That context is valuable when the video is revisited later.

## When analysis fails

Confirm that the replay is public, fully processed, within the trial limits, and available in your region. If it is still live, private, removed, or protected, use the creator's official archive instead.

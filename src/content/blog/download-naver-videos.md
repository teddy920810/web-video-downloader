Naver hosts news clips, creator videos, entertainment, and live programming. Availability differs by region and by the publisher's settings, so a workflow that works for one public clip may not work for another.

## Start with permission and access

Use downloads only for your own uploads, licensed material, or content whose owner has given you permission. If a video is behind a login, subscription, regional restriction, or other access control, use the publisher's official viewing experience.

Open the video in its canonical page rather than copying an embedded player from another site. A clean page URL makes it easier to tell which service owns the content and whether a download option is offered.

## Check whether the link is supported

Our current web trial focuses on YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram. Naver links are not yet a guaranteed input, so do not assume that a successful page load means a media file can be prepared.

If you are evaluating another tool, look for a clear support list, a privacy policy, and an explanation of where files are processed. Avoid services that ask for your Naver password or request unnecessary browser permissions.

## Choose the safest offline route

When Naver or the publisher provides an official offline or download control, use it. For a downloadable file supplied by the owner, save the original rather than re-encoding it repeatedly. Re-encoding can reduce quality and make subtitles harder to preserve.

## Common reasons a video cannot be saved

- The publisher has disabled downloads.
- The clip requires a logged-in or paid account.
- The stream is region restricted.
- The page exposes only an embedded player, not a public media URL.
- The recording is still live and has not been archived.

## Plan for the desktop release

The upcoming desktop product will be the place for broader platform workflows. Until a platform is explicitly listed as supported, treat it as unsupported and use the official service rather than repeatedly retrying the web trial.
